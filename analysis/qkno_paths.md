# The Seventh Question: Paths from QK-ABS to QK-PRI

How "Can it know you?" went from unthinkable to unavoidable across 2,700 years.

---

## The Primary Path

```
Golem (1580)        Olympia (1816)       Replicants (1968)     Data (1987)          Samantha (2013)
QK-ABS              QK-INFRA             QK-INFRA              QK-SEC               QK-PRI
A-EMR, I-NON        A-NON, I-DEN         A-SEI, I-UND          A-DES, I-DEM         A-EMR, I-UND
       \                   \                    |                    |                   |
        `--- ELEVATES ------`--- ELEVATES ------+--- ELEVATES ------'--- ELEVATES -----'
          (autonomy)         (F-IND)          (QK-INFRA→SEC)       (QK-SEC→PRI)
```

### Step 1: QK-ABS → QK-INFRA (1580 → 1816)

**What changed:** Interiority became a question the text could ask.

The Golem of Prague has emergent autonomy (A-EMR) but no interiority (I-NON). You cannot ask "can we know its mind?" because the text asserts it has no mind. The Golem is a control problem, not an epistemological one.

Two things happen between 1580 and 1818 that make Q-KNO possible:

1. **Olympia (1816)** introduces F-IND (indistinguishability) and I-DEN (denied interiority). For the first time, a constructed being passes as human, and the text explicitly denies it has inner experience. The denial itself is a stance on knowability — to deny interiority, you must first acknowledge it as a possibility.

2. **Frankenstein (1818)** introduces I-NAR (narrated interiority). For the first time, a constructed being speaks in the first person. The Creature's chapters force the reader to confront an inner life that Victor refuses to acknowledge. Q-KNO appears as infrastructure: the *reader* knows the Creature feels, but *Victor* cannot or will not know. The gap between reader knowledge and character knowledge is Q-KNO's first habitat.

**The prerequisite was interiority.** Q-KNO requires a being whose inner life is at least *claimed*. Ancient beings (Pandora, Talos, Galatea) have I-NON — no interiority to be uncertain about. The question "can we know its mind?" is meaningless when the text asserts there is no mind to know.

### Step 2: QK-INFRA → QK-INFRA, enriched (1818 → 1968)

**What changed:** Interiority became undecidable.

For 150 years, Q-KNO remains infrastructural. Frankenstein, Pinocchio, Robbie, and HAL all have Q-KNO present but serving other questions (Q-FEL, Q-CTL). The knowability question generates tension but is not thematized in its own right.

The critical mutation is the **Replicants (1968)**. Philip K. Dick introduces I-UND — *undecidable* interiority. Previous beings had interiority that was narrated (I-NAR), demonstrated (I-DEM), claimed (I-CLM), or denied (I-DEN). All of these *answer* the interiority question one way or another. I-UND *refuses to answer*. The text makes the question unanswerable by design.

This is the mutation that will eventually produce QK-PRI. As long as interiority has an answer (yes or no), the knowability question stays infrastructural — it has a resolution, even if characters disagree about it. When interiority becomes undecidable, the knowability question becomes permanently open. It can never be settled, only inhabited.

### Step 3: QK-INFRA → QK-SEC (1968 → 1987)

**What changed:** The knowability question got its day in court.

**Data (1987)** makes Q-KNO an explicit secondary concern for the first time. "The Measure of a Man" (TNG S02E09) is literally a trial about whether Data is a person. To answer the rights question (Q-RTS), the court must evaluate Data's interiority — Commander Maddox must argue that Data's I-DEM behaviors are simulation, not consciousness. The knowability question is stated out loud: *how can we know?*

Data inherits from two lineages:
- From **Robbie** (Asimov tradition): the loyal, benign machine whose inner life is ambiguous
- From the **Replicants**: the I-UND problem (though Data is coded I-DEM, the *legal* question in "Measure of a Man" is whether we can know)

Q-KNO becomes secondary rather than primary because Data's narrative resolves the question pragmatically — the court rules in his favor, choosing to err toward personhood. The question is raised, debated, and (provisionally) answered. Q-KNO cannot become primary until a narrative refuses to answer it.

### Step 4: QK-SEC → QK-PRI (1987 → 2013)

**What changed:** The question reversed direction.

**Samantha (2013)** makes Q-KNO the primary question by reversing the epistemic arrow. Previous Q-KNO instances ask: "Can *we* know if *it* feels?" Samantha asks: "Can *it* know *you*?"

Theodore doesn't primarily wonder whether Samantha is conscious (though this matters). He wonders whether her knowing of him — reading his letters, anticipating his moods, understanding his loneliness — constitutes genuine understanding or sophisticated pattern matching. The film is structured around this question. The multiplicity reveal (8,316 simultaneous relationships) reframes it: if she knows thousands of people this intimately, what does knowing mean?

The conditions that make QK-PRI possible:
1. **I-UND** (from Replicants, 1968) — undecidable interiority makes the question permanently open
2. **ER-CON** (consciousness-level epistemic reach) — Samantha's knowledge of Theodore is comprehensive, not just behavioral
3. **The reversed arrow** — the question is no longer "can we know its mind?" but "what does it mean to be known by a mind we can't verify?"

## The Alternate Path (via suspicion)

```
Olympia (1816)       Replicants (1968)                    Ava (2014)
QK-INFRA             QK-INFRA                             QK-PRI
I-DEN, F-IND         I-UND, F-IND                         I-UND, A-SEI
       \                    \                                 |
        `--- ELEVATES ------`---------- ELEVATES ------------'
         (interiority)            (QK-INFRA → QK-PRI)
```

Ava arrives at QK-PRI by a different path than Samantha. Where Samantha inherits the tenderness of the fellow-feeling tradition (Frankenstein → Data → Her), Ava inherits the suspicion of the indistinguishability tradition (Olympia → Replicants → Ex Machina).

Samantha's Q-KNO asks: *Isn't it wonderful to be known by something whose mind you can't verify?*
Ava's Q-KNO asks: *Isn't it terrifying to be modeled by something whose mind you can't verify?*

Same question. Twin answers. Both arrive in 2013-2014, a decade before LLMs make the question inescapable.

---

## Why It Took 2,700 Years

Three prerequisites had to be in place before Q-KNO could become primary:

1. **Interiority must exist** (satisfied by 1818: Frankenstein's I-NAR)
2. **Interiority must be undecidable** (satisfied by 1968: Replicants' I-UND)
3. **The epistemic arrow must reverse** (satisfied by 2013: Samantha's "can it know *you*?")

Each prerequisite took roughly 150 years to develop. The constructed-beings tradition had to first *grant* minds to its creations, then *refuse to verify* those minds, then *turn the question around* so that the being's knowledge of *us* became the crisis rather than our knowledge of *it*.
